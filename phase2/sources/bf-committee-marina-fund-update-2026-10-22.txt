Marina Fund Challenges Over Time
 •   1970s-1990s: boater fees kept low, added non-profit leases, capped
     landfill and expanded parkland  more cost, same revenue
 •   2000s: structural deficits driven by:
     • Post-2000 and 2008 economic downturns:
         Users pull boats out of the water.
         Restaurant and hotel revenue decrease.

     • Safety issues 2015-2019 led to berther exodus / Hs Lordships closing

     • Aging infrastructure + no capital reserves => $163M in unfunded needs

 • 2020s: Covid-19: major hit to hotel/restaurant revenue

 • External financial support needed to operate Waterfront starting in FY22

Marina Fund Key Factors | Loss of Doubletree Hotel Revenue
 • DoubleTree is the largest single contributor to the Marina Fund, expected to cover
   15% of waterfront expenses (~$1.2M/year in revenue)
 • Hotel financial trouble:
    • Hotel generated $1.4M in lease revenue in 2019.
    • Hotel has been losing money every year since 2020.
    • Loan went into special servicing in 2025, with lender controlling expenditures.
    • Hotel stopped paying regular rent in March 2025.
    • Hotel paid their full balance in Sept 2025: $964,680.
    • No rent has been paid to the City since Dec 2025.
    • Hotel currently owes approx. $1M (10 months of base rent, 4 quarters of % rent).

 • Future payments uncertain.
 • Marina Fund FY27-28 budget included $840k in projected revenue (approx. 30%
   of required rent).

Marina Fund Key Factors | Loss of Parking Revenue
 • Council approved new parking fees at 3 Waterfront lots in June 2025:
    • $1/hour, max of $6/day or $500/year at J/K and South Cove East and West lots
    • Applies to 263 parking spaces. 987 spaces would continue to be free.
    • The Waterfront has 2,235 parking spaces. Other spaces include paid lots at hotel and
      launch ramp, tenant and boater lots.

 • Subsequently, BCDC required City to apply for a permit to apply these fees.
   (First time in BCDC history.)
 • In August 2026, BCDC Commission rejected the City’s request for an
   administrative permit for $1/hr parking program in South Cove area lots.
 • BCDC instructed the City to re-apply for a “major permit” – a process
   typically reserved for much larger projects with high impact to the Bay.
 • Marina Fund FY27-28 budget included $335k in projected net parking
   revenue over two years.

Marina Fund | Anticipated Deficit in FY28
• Revenue is coming in lower than projected.
    • Doubletree has not paid since Dec 2025. Staff budgeted $840k in revenue in FY27 and FY28 (representing 30% of their
      yearly payments).
    • Paid parking in 3 South Cove lots has not materialized. Staff budgeted $335k in revenue in FY27 and FY28.
• Already exhausted emergency funding for FY27 in October; 8 months still remaining, with El Nino
  winter on the horizon.
    • Spending emergency funds on South Cove seawall area for sinkhole.
    • Spending emergency funds on flood barriers for Marina.
• Maintenance of an appropriate reserve balance. Typical reserve balances for small funds like the
  Marina Fund are at least 15%.
    • We're attempting to set aside at least 8% (or approximately $500k).
• Results in $1.2M+ needed in reductions, cost shifts or new revenue
    • Includes $545k projected fund deficit in FY28, $500k prudent reserve, and $150k+ additional emergency funding in
      FY27 and FY28.
