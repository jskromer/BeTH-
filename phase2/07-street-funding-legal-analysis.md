## 1. Measure FF (SAFE STREETS)

**Identity.** Measure FF is the November 5, 2024 SAFE STREETS parcel tax, City fund **166 "Measure FF – Street Repair"**. It is *not* fund 164 "Measure FF – Public Safety" (a 2020 fire and emergency services tax), and *not* the ACFR's "2009 Measure FF Library" fund (2008 library bonds). BeTH's earlier crosswalk conflated these and is corrected.

**Legal authority.** A citizens' initiative, passed by majority vote. Adopted as Ordinance 7,952-N.S. on December 10, 2024 and codified as BMC Chapter 7.11. A special tax under Cal. Const. art. XIIIA, §4 and charter-city authority (art. XI, §5) (7.11.010(C)). It runs 14 years from January 1, 2025 (7.11.030(A); Section 5). [`sources/measure-ff-initiative-council-2024-07-09.pdf`, PDF pp.5–15; transcription in `sources/measure-ff-ordinance-transcription.md`; FY2027 rate report p.2]

**Permitted uses.** Proceeds are held "in a special fund to be used only for" these purposes (7.11.010(E)–(J)):
- repair, repaving and reconstruction, safety improvements, and environmental enhancements of streets, sidewalks and pedestrian paths;
- staff costs, audit costs, a ten-yearly Transportation Element update and a review of the project delivery process;
- public art, at no more than 1.75% of project budgets.

It may not fund bicycle-specific safety improvements on Hopkins Street between McGee and Gilman.

**Allocation fixed by voters.** "Approximately" 45% streets, 15% sidewalks and paths (until that backlog is eliminated), 30% safety, and 10% environmental enhancements, County fees and other permitted uses (7.11.020(C)). The allocation lapses once the citywide average PCI reaches 70 (7.11.020(D)). That subsection cross-references "7.11.030(C)", which concerns external funding, so it is probably a drafting error. Current PCI is 57.

**Council's authority to redirect.** Council *may* amend the ordinance without voters, **except** in ways that alter the allocations of 7.11.020, expand permitted uses inconsistently with the measure's intent, or reduce the budgeting requirement of 7.11.030 (Section 7(a)(2)–(4), PDF p.15). Within the streets share, Council allocates and approves which streets (7.11.010(D)). Every Measure FF paving project must include concurrent safety improvements unless Council adopts a written finding based on substantial evidence (7.11.060(B)).

**Restricted, committed, assigned or unrestricted?** Restricted: a special tax for restricted purposes under enabling legislation. No audited balance exists yet. The FY2025 ACFR predates collections, so the first audited figure will be in the FY2026 ACFR.

**Projects that could be deferred to free money for paving.** None. Deferring a Measure FF safety, sidewalk or environmental project does not make that money available for paving, because the allocation binds it.

**Amount potentially available for paving.** **$0** beyond the adopted plan. The streets share is already at the ordinance's approximately-45%. The only increment is the streets share of revenue not yet appropriated (~45% of the $1.35M FY2027 increase), plus any unprogrammed FY2026 receipts. That second amount is unknown.

## 2. CIP Fund 501 (Capital Improvement Fund)

**Legal authority.** A capital projects fund financed chiefly by an annual General Fund transfer. General Fund revenues "may be used for any general government purpose at the discretion of Council" (adoption packet p.167).
- **Appropriations:** made annually through the budget and the Annual Appropriation Ordinance. "Any unused funds are re-appropriated to the following fiscal year until the project is completed" (FY2025 ACFR, PDF p.180, printed p.146).
- **Paving policy:** Resolution 70,456-N.S. (July 26, 2022) commits about $8 million a year of General Fund money to paving, adjusted for inflation. It runs through fund 501 as "Street Rehab PCI Improvements" ($8,487,200 in FY2027) (street plan pp.2–3, Table 1A; adoption packet pp.167, 215). This is a Council policy, amendable by Council, but see the maintenance-of-effort floor below.

**Permitted uses.** Capital projects as Council appropriates them. No voter or external restriction attaches to the General Fund transfer.

**Classification (audited, June 30, 2025).** FY2025 ACFR, PDF p.75 (printed p.40):

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|

| Notes receivable (loans; not cash) | $7,771,575 |
| Liabilities | $1,487,942 |
| Fund balance: **restricted** | $7,771,575 (equal to the notes receivable) |
| Fund balance: committed / unassigned | $0 / $0 |
| Fund balance: **assigned** | $16,060,094 |
| of which encumbered (contracts and purchase orders) | $4,491,447 (ACFR PDF p.142, printed p.108) |
| **Unencumbered assigned** (derived) | **$11,568,647** |

"Assigned" means "intended to be used by the City for specific purposes that are neither restricted nor committed through City Council budgetary action … Intent is expressed by the City Council or City Manager, to which the City Council has delegated the authority" (ACFR PDF p.103, printed p.69). Encumbrances "are reported in assigned fund balance unless the purchase order relates to restricted or committed resources" (ACFR PDF p.180). The unencumbered $11.57M is the cash behind capital appropriations carried forward, paving among them. It is not free money, and it is sixteen months old.

The City's own description: "current CIP funding is entirely pre-allocated for historical programs such as those in Public Works, there is no CIP funding available for IT in FY 2027 and FY 2028" (p.239). Of the roughly $4.95M annual General Fund baseline, about $4.03M goes to capital and the rest to personnel, debt service and other costs (p.167).

**Council's authority to redirect.** Yes, without voters. Council amends the FY2027 Annual Appropriation Ordinance. Encumbered amounts are contract obligations and cannot move without changing or cancelling the contracts.

**Projects that could be deferred or cancelled (FY2027–28 appropriations).** Every non-paving fund 501 project appears in `data/street-funding-sources.json` → `deferral_candidates`, with its amounts and a nature label. FY2027 and FY2028 hold $1,473,000 each:

BeTH has **not** assessed what deferring any of these would cost in condition, safety, legal exposure or contracts, and this list is not a recommendation. Most are repairs to existing City buildings.

**Amount potentially available for paving.**
- New money, FY2027–28: **at most $2,946,000**, the sum of every non-paving fund 501 appropriation in those two years ($2,916,000 excluding the JPA contribution). It is a ceiling.
- Planned FY2029–31 non-paving: $4,433,000 (not appropriated).
- One-time carryover: **unknown**, at most $11,568,647 at June 30, 2025.

## 3. The constraint that runs the other way: Measure FF maintenance of effort

BMC 7.11.030(B): "If the City Council fails to adopt a budget with at least $15.3 million in funding for street maintenance, from sources other than this tax, and adjusted for inflation, per Resolution No. 70,456-N.S. … the City Council shall not be authorized to impose the special tax for the following two years." The floor is suspended in any year Council draws $1 million or more from the Stabilization Reserve to adopt a budget, or under a disaster or emergency declaration (7.11.030(D)). It is reduced if an external street funding source in the 2023–24 budget declines (7.11.030(C)). Council cannot lower it without voters (Section 7(a)(4)).

- **FY2027 floor (BeTH estimate, Moderate):** about $16.23 million. That is $15.3M × 1.0609, the inflation factor the City applied to the $8M policy component ($8,487,200 ÷ $8,000,000). The ordinance does not state the base year or the index.
- **FY2027 non-FF street funding:** $18,742,503 in the street plan (Table 1A, p.2), or $17,742,503 excluding the $1M Clean Storm drainage allocation.
- **Headroom:** about $1.5–2.5 million.
- **Unknown:** whether FY2027 draws on the Stabilization Reserve.

**Implication.** Moving money *into* paving only raises the floor-counted total. Moving more than the headroom *out* of non-FF paving (most of which is fund 501) would risk about $16.7 million a year of Measure FF for two years. Fund 501's paving money is therefore effectively locked in place.
