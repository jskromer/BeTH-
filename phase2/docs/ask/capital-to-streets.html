How much capital funding could Berkeley redirect to streets?

Short answer

Little can be shown to be available. Fund 501 could free at most $2,946,000 over two years, only by delaying every other project it pays for; that is a budget ceiling, not cash. Whether Measure FF could add more is an open legal question.

What you should know
  • The $2,946,000 is money budgeted for other projects, not money known to be unspent. Public records do not show how much is already under contract or spent.
  • Freeing it means delaying roof, building, pool, sidewalk and other repairs. BeTH has not assessed what those delays would cost.
  • Measure FF says "approximately" 45% for streets. Whether that allows more paving without a vote has not been settled by the City Attorney or Council.

The full answer and BeTH's reading

From CIP Fund 501: a gross conditional ceiling of $2,946,000 of FY2027-FY2028 appropriations, or $2,916,000 excluding a contractual Joint Powers Agreement contribution. It is reached only by deferring or cancelling every other project the fund pays for, and neither figure is verified available cash. 88% of the fund's FY2027 money ($10,412,200) already goes to paving. A one-time amount from unspent carried-over appropriations may exist but cannot be measured from public records; its ceiling at June 30, 2025 was $11,568,647. From Measure FF: the adopted program already puts 45.6% into street rehabilitation, against a voter-set allocation of "approximately" 45% that Council cannot alter without a vote. Whether more could go to paving without a vote is an unresolved legal question, and BeTH states no amount.

  • For scale, the two figures measure different things. The City estimates that reaching an average PCI of 70 would take about $8 million a year of additional spending beyond its modeled Plan + Measure FF scenario. Fund 501's non-paving capital is $1,473,000 a year of budgeted projects, mostly repairs to existing City buildings, that could be freed only by deferring them; it is not available cash. Redirecting it would not close the modeled gap. (BeTH's confidence: Moderate)
    The other side: This covers only fund 501 and Measure FF. Other capital funds, unspent carryovers, the General Fund itself and delivery-cost savings are not assessed here and could change the total.

Scope: CIP Fund 501 and Measure FF only. Other capital funds (sewer, storm water, zero waste, parks tax, state and county transportation funds, internal service funds) are not yet assessed here.

Open questions (6) · what is unsettled or not yet known
  • BeTH has not assessed what it would cost to delay the roof, building, pool, sidewalk and other repairs that fund 501 pays for, including legal, contract and safety consequences.
  • Public records do not show how much of the FY2027-FY2028 budget for these projects is already under contract or spent. The ceilings are budget amounts, not cash known to be available.
  • The $11,568,647 one-time ceiling is from June 30, 2025, sixteen months ago, and already backs projects carried forward from earlier years. How much of it is still uncommitted is unknown.
  • For Measure FF, BeTH gives no dollar amount because the legal questions on the Measure FF page are unresolved.
  • Measure FF requires the City to keep spending a minimum amount of other money on street maintenance. BeTH estimated that minimum because the City has not published its own calculation, and the ordinance does not state the base year or inflation index. The minimum limits moving paving money out of streets, not into them.
  • Changes Council made at the June 23, 2026 meeting are not in the budget document BeTH used. The FY2027 Annual Appropriation Ordinance, the controlling record, is not in BeTH's sources.

How the numbers are worked out (4)
  • Gross conditional deferral ceiling: fund 501 non-paving appropriations, FY2027 + FY2028: $1,473,000 + $1,473,000 = $2,946,000
    Appropriations, not verified available cash. Reached only by deferring or cancelling every one of them.
    Using: Of which all other fund 501 projects; FY2028, all other fund 501 projects.
    fund:501/cip/fy2027/other + fund:501/cip/fy2028/other
  • Deferral ceiling excluding the contractual JPA contribution: $2,946,000 − $30,000 = $2,916,000
    Not verified available cash.
    Using: Gross conditional deferral ceiling: fund 501 non-paving appropriations, FY2027 + FY2028; Of which a contractual Joint Powers Agreement contribution.
    c1 − fund:501/cip/fy2027-28/contractual-jpa
  • Share of fund 501 FY2027 money already in paving: $10,412,200 ÷ $11,885,200 = 87.6%
    Using: Of which Streets (paving); Fund 501 (CIP Fund) FY2027 capital program.
    fund:501/cip/fy2027/paving ÷ fund:501/cip/fy2027/total
  • Unencumbered assigned fund balance, June 30, 2025 (ceiling for one-time carryover): $16,060,094 − $4,491,447 = $11,568,647
    Backs capital appropriations already carried forward, paving among them. Not free money.
    Using: Fund 501 assigned fund balance, June 30, 2025; Fund 501 encumbrances outstanding, June 30, 2025.
    fund:501/acfr/2025/assigned − fund:501/acfr/2025/encumbrances
