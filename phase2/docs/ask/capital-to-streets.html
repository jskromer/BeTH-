How much capital funding could Berkeley redirect to streets?

From Measure FF, nothing beyond the adopted plan without a vote of the people. From CIP Fund 501, at most $2,946,000 of FY2027-FY2028 money, and only by deferring or cancelling every other project the fund pays for; 88% of its FY2027 money ($10,412,200) already goes to paving. A one-time amount from unspent carried-over appropriations may exist but cannot be measured from public records; its ceiling at June 30, 2025 was $11,568,647.

Arithmetic

  • Fund 501 non-paving appropriations, FY2027 + FY2028 (ceiling for new paving money): fund:501/cip/fy2027/other + fund:501/cip/fy2028/other = $2,946,000
  • Share of fund 501 FY2027 money already in paving: fund:501/cip/fy2027/paving / fund:501/cip/fy2027/total = 87.6%
  • Unencumbered assigned fund balance, June 30, 2025 (ceiling for one-time carryover): fund:501/acfr/2025/assigned - fund:501/acfr/2025/encumbrances = $11,568,647 Backs capital appropriations already carried forward, paving among them. Not free money.

BeTH's reading

  • The capital money Council could redirect to paving without voters is small relative to the paving gap: the City estimates PCI 70 needs about $8 million a year more, while fund 501's non-paving capital is $1,473,000 a year, mostly repairs to existing City buildings. (Moderate)
    Counterargument: This covers only fund 501 and Measure FF. Other capital funds, unspent carryovers, the General Fund itself and delivery-cost savings are not assessed here and could change the total.
