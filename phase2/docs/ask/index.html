Capital budget

Can capital money fix our streets?

Berkeley's capital budget pays for buildings, parks, sewers and streets. How much of it could move to paving, and what would have to give way?

Little can be shown to be available. Fund 501 could free at most $2,946,000 over two years, only by delaying every other project it pays for; that is a budget ceiling, not cash. Whether Measure FF could add more is an open legal question.

Measure FF

Could Measure FF pave more streets?

Voters approved a street tax with a fixed split between paving, sidewalks, safety and the environment. Is there room to pave more?

Unclear. Voters fixed Measure FF's split at about 45% for streets, and the adopted plan already puts 45.6% into street repaving. Whether more could go to paving without a vote is an open legal question.

Street condition

How bad are Berkeley's streets?

The citywide average hides a lot. See how many miles of street score below the line Measure FF set as its goal.

Worse than the average suggests. The 2024 average was PCI 56 out of 100, but 88.5 miles of streets, 42% of the network, were below PCI 50.

Street budget

What does the big streets figure include?

A figure often quoted for street spending covers five years, not two. Here is what is in it, and what has actually been budgeted.

It is a five-year figure, not a two-year one. The $139,356,147 covers FY2027 through FY2031; the two-year amount is $58,211,462.

How BeTH answers

Only City records

Budgets, audited financial reports, the Measure FF ordinance and the City's pavement report. Every figure links to its page.

Checked by a program

Cited pages must exist, figures must match the records, quotes must appear on the cited page and the arithmetic must add up. If anything fails, the answer is not shown.

Reviewed by a person

The last step is an independent reviewer reading every source. That has not happened yet for these answers, and each page says so.
