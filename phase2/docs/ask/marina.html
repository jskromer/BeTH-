Waterfront

Is Berkeley's Marina paying for itself?

Short answer

No. Audited FY2024 operating costs exceeded revenue by $401,892, staff's unaudited FY2026 figures balance only with about $1.4 million of General Fund support, and staff project a deficit by FY2028.

Does the Marina cover its costs?

Not consistently. In audited FY2024, operating expenses exceeded operating revenue by $401,892. Audited FY2025 shows a much larger operating loss, $5,979,084, in a year the fund also received a $7,689,069 State capital grant for dock work; the statements do not show how much of the year's expense that work accounts for. Staff's unaudited FY2026 figures balance only because revenue includes about $1.4 million of General Fund subsidy; without it the shortfall is about $1,122,408.

If not, who pays the difference?

Other City money, in several different forms: federal pandemic aid in FY2022 ($1.4 million) and FY2023 ($1.15 million); cost shifts to other funds ($800,000 in FY2024); reduced City overhead charges and Workers' Compensation "holidays" ($850,000 in FY2025, $360,000 in FY2026); a General Fund transfer of about $1.4 million in FY2026; and a $2,477,099 loan from the General Fund, still owed at June 30, 2025. Since FY2026 the Parks Tax pays $1.579 million a year of waterfront park and landscape costs. Staff told the committee there is no General Fund money for FY2027-FY2028.

What would it take to make the waterfront financially sustainable?

Staff estimate at least $1.2 million of cuts, cost shifts or new revenue just to reach FY2028 with a small reserve, before any capital work. Separately, the waterfront has $163,344,000 of unfunded infrastructure needs, and the City's update names no funding source for them. When the fund was set up in 1964, berth fees and hotel and restaurant leases were expected to pay for the whole waterfront; how much of its public parks, paths and services boaters and tenants should carry, and how much the wider city should, is a policy choice, not an accounting fact.

What you should know
  • FY2026 numbers are City staff figures, not audited. The audited report for FY2026 is not yet published.
  • The DoubleTree hotel owed about $1 million in rent in October 2026. That is rent still owed, not written off; whether it will be paid is unknown.
  • The $5.5 million Workers' Compensation loan cannot be traced to the Marina Fund in the audited statements: the FY2024 audit shows the money going to the General Fund.

The full answer and BeTH's reading

The Marina Fund (fund 608) is meant to pay for the whole waterfront from berth fees and leases. In audited FY2024 it had an operating loss of $401,892; in audited FY2025, $5,979,084, a year distorted by State-funded dock work. Staff's unaudited FY2026 figures show revenue of $8,673,012 against expenditures of $8,395,420, but the revenue includes about $1.4 million of General Fund subsidy. Staff project losses of $1,023,955 in FY2027 and $1,045,594 in FY2028, leaving the fund $545,586 in deficit. The DoubleTree hotel owed about $1 million in rent in October 2026.

  • At current fees, leases and service levels the Marina Fund does not pay for the waterfront it runs; one-time help and support from other City funds have covered the gap since FY2022. (BeTH's confidence: Moderate)
    The other side: Much of the waterfront is public parkland, paths, restrooms, security and recreation used by everyone, not only boaters. A 2023 State review, cited by the Berkeley Decision Project and not yet verified by BeTH, estimated that boating revenue roughly covered boating costs. On that view the question is how the public parts of the waterfront should be funded, not whether boaters underpay.
  • Unpaid DoubleTree rent is the largest near-term risk to the fund: it is the largest single tenant and the budget counts on partial payment that has not arrived. (BeTH's confidence: Moderate)
    The other side: The rent is still owed, not written off, and the hotel cleared an earlier balance in September 2025. If it pays again, the projected deficit shrinks; if it does not, the loss is larger than the budget assumed.

Scope: Marina Fund (fund 608). Audited figures are from the FY2024 and FY2025 ACFRs; FY2026 figures are City staff figures (unaudited); FY2027-FY2028 are staff projections. BeTH's approved ledger for fund 608 is not changed; suspected ledger issues are flagged separately in phase2/flags/.

Open questions (10) · what is unsettled or not yet known
  • The FY2025 operating loss includes depreciation, which is not cash, and an unknown amount of dock and pile work paid for by the State capital grant. FY2025 is therefore not a clean measure of day-to-day operations.
  • The $2,477,099 owed to the General Fund at June 30, 2025 was "expected to be repaid within the next fiscal year". Whether it was repaid, and whether it is connected to the $5.5 million authorization, is not shown in any document BeTH holds.
  • The FY2024 audit shows $5,500,000 leaving the Workers' Compensation Fund and going to the General Fund. The Marina Fund recorded no transfer in and owed nothing to another fund at June 30, 2024. The audit's transfer note lists the row under the Equipment Replacement Fund while its fund statement shows the money leaving Workers' Compensation, an inconsistency inside the audit report itself. Whether waterfront hotel tax has been used to repay the loan, as the 2023 authorization intended, is not shown.
  • City documents give different amounts for General Fund support in FY2026. BeTH has not reconciled them to one adopted figure.
  • The audited FY2025 line "Marina operations and maintenance" ($5,329,258) and staff's FY2026 "Berthers" ($3,971,848) measure different things; what the audited line includes beyond berth rent is not stated.
  • Staff's FY2026 revenue components add to one dollar less than the stated total. The difference is in the City's table and is recorded, not corrected.
  • DoubleTree arrears: about $890,000 in September 2026 (nine months of base rent, three quarters of percentage rent) and about $1 million in October 2026 (ten months, four quarters). Staff counted on $840,000 of DoubleTree rent over FY2027-FY2028, described as approximately 30% of rent; the September packet also describes the assumption as at least 35%. Whether the City has recorded an allowance for doubtful accounts or started lease enforcement is not established.
  • Costs caused by boating and costs of public waterfront services are not separated in any audited statement. The only estimate BeTH knows of is a 2023 State report, cited by a third party and not yet obtained by BeTH.
  • Staff expenditure totals and audited operating expenses are measured differently (for example, staff figures are on a budget basis and audited figures include depreciation), so audited and staff years are not directly comparable.
  • The State boating loan balance ($5,415,120 at June 30, 2025) is still being repaid from the Marina Fund.
