## P1 — Blocking

### Q1. Where did the Workers' Compensation Fund's $5,500,000 actually go?

**Conflict.** Eric: Workers' Compensation → **General Fund**, FY2024, no receivable recorded, no repayment documented. BeTH ledger: Workers' Compensation → **Marina (608)** as `loan_in`.

**What BeTH verified independently in the FY2025 ACFR:**
- `5,500,000` appears **nowhere** in the document.
- Note 4.A *Due To/From Other Funds* (p.79) at 2025-06-30 lists **only** $2,477,099 due from Marina Operations. No Workers' Compensation receivable at any size. Total $23,193,295.
- Marina audited transfers **in**, FY2024: **$0**.
- Workers' Compensation audited transfers **out**, FY2024: **$5,500,000**.

**Why BeTH's own entry is suspect.** BeTH appears to have recorded the Council's stated *purpose* ("Waterfront D and E dock replacement") as the *destination*. Its 608 obligation is dated `as_of 2023-07-25` — the authorization date — not a balance-sheet date, unlike the properly dated $2,477,099 entry.

**Documents needed:** FY2024 ACFR interfund note and fund statements; FY2024/FY2025 Workers' Compensation fund statements; any executed loan agreement or promissory note; any repayment schedule.

**Until resolved:** publish neither figure. Do not change BeTH data on the strength of a third-party analysis.

### Q2. Is the Workers' Compensation Fund solvent, and what happens to the claims it owes?

FY2025 audited: $0 revenue, $11,983,900 expenses, ending net position **−$9,752,938**, after two consecutive contribution holidays. A further **$5,187,019** is budgeted out to the General Fund in FY2026.

**Questions.** What is the actuarial claims liability? What is the funding plan? Does moving money out of a negative-net-position insurance fund to close a General Fund gap create a future liability that exceeds the gap it closes? Were the holidays actuarially supported?

**Documents needed:** actuarial valuation; FY2026/FY2027 budget treatment; any Council report authorizing the holidays.
