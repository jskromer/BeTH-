 Recommendation
A dollar of maintenance early in a street’s life-cycle saves $8 later due to avoided rehabilitation and/or
                         reconstruction costs associated with failing streets

Refer to the FY 2023-25 biennial budget $4.7 million
General Fund in FY 2024-25 for street paving—bringing the
total street paving annual budget to $20 million—to begin
to improve the pavement condition

Why?
 New State Green Infrastructure mandate will increase paving
  costs per acre
 More funding enables more low-cost bike/pedestrian
  infrastructure to be added at time of paving
 Delay leads to exponentially higher costs later
 “At Risk” Streets Harm All Users
Berkeley’s streets were rated at an average of 55 out of 100 (Pavement Condition Index) in 2023,
                                    meaning they are “at risk”



                                      Drivers pay $1,049 annually (according to TRIP, a national
                                       transportation research group) in vehicle repair costs,
                                       accelerated vehicle deterioration and depreciation,
                                       increased maintenance costs, and additional fuel
                                       consumption; this hurts lower-income drivers more
                                      Safety concerns for bicyclists and pedestrians who suffer
                                       injuries due to potholes and uneven pavement



                                                    Source: Berkeley City Auditor, Rocky Road: Berkeley Streets at Risk and Significantly Underfunded, Nov. 19, 2020
