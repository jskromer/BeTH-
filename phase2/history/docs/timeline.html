This timeline sets City Council decisions about streets and infrastructure next to what happened afterwards. Each card answers six questions from the public record and says plainly when a record has not yet been collected.

How to read it
  • Placing a decision before an outcome does not mean the decision caused it. Where the City attributes a result to a decision, the card says so and names who made the claim.
  • "Alternatives" are only those documented at the time: failed motions and options in the recommendation. Hindsight scenarios are kept separate and none are included yet.
  • Votes are copied exactly as recorded. "All Ayes" is not expanded into names.
  • No scores, rankings or ratings of officials.

What happened, in brief

A short account built only from the records below. Open any claim's evidence to see the page it rests on.

From FY2014 to FY2020, Berkeley budgeted roughly $4.9 million to $11.3 million a year for street rehabilitation. The high years came from one-time bond money (Measure M from 2012, Measure T1 from 2016). Recurring money stayed between about $3.5 million and $7 million.

Street condition barely moved. The City reported a PCI of 58 in 2011 and 59 in 2018. The Metropolitan Transportation Commission's three-year averages stayed between 56 and 60 from 2010 to 2022.

In January 2022 Council chose between two paving plans of similar cost from the same funding: the Equity Alternative ($38.4 million, 15.94 miles, more residential streets) and the Commission's Arterial Alternative ($40.0 million, 15.49 miles). The choice was about which streets, not how much money. At that funding level staff projected a PCI of 52 by 2023 and the Commission 49.1 by 2027.

Council then raised recurring funding. The June 2022 budget added General Fund paving money. In July 2022 a policy committed $8 million a year more on top of an "existing General Fund street paving budget of $7.3 million". That $7.3 million was mostly state and county money: only $1,925,000 of it was General Fund.

Did new money add to street funding or replace it?

What the record shows so far. Each item states its evidence and how far it goes.

FY2013-FY2014. The General Fund's Capital Improvement Fund contribution to streets capital fell from $2.8M in FY2013 to $1.9M in FY2014, the year Measure M bond money first appears in the street budget, and stayed at $1.925M a year through at least FY2027.

Reading: Timing is consistent with partial displacement of General Fund money by bond money. The record does not say why the contribution fell; the Auditor does not attribute it to Measure M. Status: possible displacement; cause not established.

July 2022. Res. 70,456-N.S. describes "the existing General Fund street paving budget of $7.3 million". The January 2022 plan's $7,275,303 baseline was all sources: only $1,925,000 of it was General Fund; the rest was gas tax, SB1, Measure BB and Measure F.

Reading: The $15.3M figure that later became Measure FF's maintenance-of-effort floor ("from sources other than this tax") was built on an all-sources baseline, though the resolution called it General Fund.
