Berkeley Budget × Audit Accountability

Rationalizing the FY2027–FY2028 Proposed Budget against the City Auditor's recommendation record.

Berkeley Transparency Hub · neutral scoreboard · residents decide
The core question this page answers: the budget cuts ~$30M/yr and ~164 positions. Are the dollars that stay — and the ones cut — tied to goals the City is actually accountable for? The Auditor has issued 384 recommendations across 54 audits on exactly that: efficiency, internal controls, and accountability. This page joins the two.

1The pattern: balanced on paper, year after year

Berkeley has reported a General Fund deficit every year since 2019 and closed each one with one-time measures — reserves, ARPA, transfers, fund balance, vacancy savings — without resolving the structural gap. The FY27–28 budget repeats the pattern: a paper surplus that depends on a November 2026 sales-tax measure and $2.1M of one-time money (dropping to $0 in FY28).

2How the gap was closed

Each year's ~$30M hole is bridged by revenue re-projection (including the assumed sales-tax measure), cost shifts, debt-service and parking adjustments, and one-time resources. FY2028 carries no one-time cushion, so the sales-tax measure is the whole margin.

4What the Auditor found — and what happened to it

Of 384 recommendations, 265 were implemented (69%), but 76 were dropped (20%) and 43 remain open. Nearly half of all findings are about Efficiency & Effectiveness, and another 29% about Transparency & Accountability — precisely the levers a structural fix needs.
