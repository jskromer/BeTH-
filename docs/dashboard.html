Following the money

City finances, as the audit reports them

Berkeley's money sits in 106 legally separate funds plus 17 fiduciary funds held for others. This dashboard reads the audited fund statements: what each group of funds took in and spent, what moved between funds, what each fund holds, and how much of the record is loaded and checked. Figures are the City's; labels say which numbers are facts, which are arithmetic, and which are BeTH's reading.

Scope. These are sums of the fund columns on four statements in the ACFR (governmental funds, nonmajor combining, proprietary funds, internal service combining). They are not City-wide totals: they leave out the 17 fiduciary funds, they are added before the statements' own eliminations, and the governmental figures are on the fund basis, which excludes capital assets, long-term debt and pension liabilities that the government-wide statements include.
Years appear here as their audited fund rows are loaded.
RevenueMoney a fund earns or is given: taxes, fees, grants, charges, interest. For enterprise and internal service funds, operating revenue only; non-operating items are shown separately.
ExpendituresGovernmental funds. Money spent in the year, including capital purchases and debt principal (modified accrual). No depreciation.
Operating expensesEnterprise and internal service funds. The cost of running the service, including depreciation of assets; capital purchases and debt principal are not expenses (full accrual).
TransfersMoney moved from one City fund to another, as authorized in the budget or by Council. Not revenue for the fund that receives it, not spending for the fund that sends it.
Fund balanceGovernmental funds. Current financial resources at year end (mainly cash, investments and receivables) less the liabilities payable from them. Much of it is restricted or committed to set uses. Excludes buildings, roads and long-term debt.
Net positionEnterprise and internal service funds. Everything the fund owns less everything it owes, including capital assets, long-term debt and pension liabilities. Not comparable to fund balance.
Other financing sourcesNot revenue. Money or value a fund receives from borrowing, from selling an asset, or from signing a lease or subscription, shown in its own section of the statement below the revenue and expenditure totals. Transfers in are reported here too; this page shows them in section 3 instead.
Inception of leases and subscriptionsA lease or software subscription signed during the year (GASB 87 and 96). The whole multi-year commitment is recorded at once, as an expenditure within capital outlay and as an other financing source of the same amount. No cash changes hands in that entry, and the City takes on a liability for the future payments, so this part of capital outlay is not cash capital spending.

4How internal service funds are handled

Internal service funds (fleet, building maintenance, central services, IT, workers' compensation, liability, catastrophic loss, leave payouts, computer replacement) are the City billing itself. A department pays the fund a charge, which the department's fund records as an expenditure or expense; the internal service fund records the same dollars as operating revenue. This page handles them in four ways:

  1. Never added to the other groups. The $44.8M of internal service operating revenue Audited is mostly charges already inside the governmental and enterprise figures; adding it would count those dollars twice. It has its own panel in section 1, and no figure on this page sums it with another group.
  2. Their costs are shown as their own. Internal service operating expenses ($63.4M) are what fleet, IT, claims and the rest actually cost. When charges equal costs, the departments' expenditures already carry them. When they differ, the fund's net position absorbs the difference.
  3. Charges and costs can diverge. In FY2025 these funds billed no charges while incurring costs: Catastrophic Loss: operating revenue $0, operating expenses $7,720,055, transfers in $7,720,657; Public Liability: operating revenue $0, operating expenses $1,707,503, transfers in $2,664,292; Workers’ Compensation: operating revenue $0, operating expenses $11,983,900, transfers in $0. Their costs that year were met by transfers in or from their own balances, not by charges to the funds whose departments incurred them Derived.
  4. Transfers are transfers. Money moved into or out of an internal service fund (in $14.2M, out $7.2M) is in section 3 with every other transfer, never in revenue or expenses.

The government-wide statements fold most internal service activity into governmental activities and eliminate the internal charges; this page reads the fund statements, where they are reported separately.

1What the FY2024 audit reports, by accounting basis

Three groups, kept apart. Governmental funds measure current financial resources (fund balance); enterprise and internal service funds measure everything they own and owe, including capital assets and long-term debt (net position). Those balances are different quantities, so this page never adds them together. Internal service revenue is mostly what other City funds pay for fleet, IT, insurance and buildings; adding it to the other groups would count the same dollars twice.

8The 17 funds without records yet: fiduciary

The Fund Map counts 123 funds: the 106 above plus 17 fiduciary funds. Fiduciary funds hold money for others (retirees, special districts, business improvement districts, the school district's Measure H tax), report on their own statements, and are left out of the government-wide totals. None has a record in BeTH's data yet, so none is in any figure on this page. Names are as the statement column headings print them.
