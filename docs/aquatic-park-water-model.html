What this model is and is not

A lumped, prismatic water balance, not a hydraulic model. Synthetic tides, triangular storms and assumed weir geometry are not observations. It does not resolve separate basins, wind, stratification, sediment or groundwater. Do not use it for engineering design. The January 2023 scenario uses a design storm, not measured rainfall.

Sources: Learn About Berkeley, Aquatic Park (full sourcing and confidence labels); model specification and reference implementation; NOAA station 9414816, Berkeley; APIP 2008; City water quality fact sheet (2022). Area, storm volumes and historical tube benchmarks are taken from the specification, not independently verified. Depth, crest, invert, conductance, evaporation and salinity are assumptions or calibration parameters.

Calibration. Three constants are fitted to the City's 2008 Aquatic Park Improvement Program (APIP) study, and nothing else is tuned. The tube constant (72 cfs per root-foot of head, at 6-minute steps) gives 23 acre-feet per flood tide at 60% tube efficiency (APIP: 22). The Alternative 4B multiplier (3.3) gives 115 acre-feet (APIP: 115). The tracer mixing efficiency (0.14) gives 47 days to flush a 100-year storm under 2007 conditions (APIP: 48.5) and, with no further change, 9.5 days under Alternative 4B (APIP: 10.4). The king tide (amplitude 1.45) peaks at 7.4 ft, matching NOAA's prediction for November 25, 2026 at Berkeley. Because the constants are fitted to those figures, the model cannot independently confirm them.

A field check, November 24, 2026. The Bay ran about 1.35 ft above NOAA's prediction that week (Richmond and Alameda gauges), so the preset adds that surge. At 4:16 pm, near low tide, the lagoon gauge at the slide gates read 6.0 ft (datum MLLW or NAVD88, within 0.13 ft). With clear tubes and no inflow, a separate water balance driven by the observed Bay record holds the lagoon at only about 5.3 ft at that hour. Either about 20 cfs of extra water was entering (tubes then carrying about 125–130 cfs) or the slide gates, which were installed to set the lagoon's level, were holding it up (about 40–55 cfs). This synthetic-tide model sits lower still, about 4.5 ft at low tide, so reproducing 6.0 ft here takes a gate control level near 5.8 ft or more than 50 cfs of inflow. The storm-surge, inflow and gate-control settings in Advanced let you try each. A second gauge reading on a falling tide decides between them: each inch per hour of fall is about 59 cfs leaving.

Other limits. The flood level (default 8 ft, adjustable under Advanced) is illustrative: the City has not published a surveyed bank or flood elevation for the lagoon. It is set above 6 ft because the lagoon gauge read 6.0 ft on November 24, 2026 with no flooding reported at that hour. Above it the computed level stands for water over the paths and Bolivar Drive, not a real lagoon surface. The weir stops spilling when the lagoon stands higher than the water in the culvert. Direct rain on the lagoon is not included. Alternative 4B is modeled as larger tidal capacity with the board out, not as the gated design the APIP proposed.
