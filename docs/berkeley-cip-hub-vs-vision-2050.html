The CIP Studio and the Dashboard Referral, Compared with Vision 2050

Vision 2050 has already committed Berkeley, on paper, to multi-criteria prioritization, cost and schedule tracking, a public website, outcome indicators and semi-annual performance reports. This page sets those commitments next to what the UC Berkeley CIP studio proposed in February 2026 and what the Council referred to the City Manager on September 29, 2026.

CWhat the comparison shows

1. The referral repeats a 2022 commitment, with one important change

The 2022 Program Plan already said performance reports would go to Council semi-annually and be posted publicly. It made that machinery conditional: the program team, the Program Management Manual and the oversight committee would come "if voters approve funding." Measure L, the $650M bond, failed in November 2022 (59.42% yes against a two-thirds requirement), and no semi-annual Vision 2050 performance report has appeared in the public record since. The September 2026 referral asks for semi-annual reports again, but this time with no condition on a revenue measure. That decoupling is the substantive change. High on the texts; Moderate that no semi-annual Vision 2050 report has been published, since this is an absence in the public record.

2. Everyone tracks inputs; Vision 2050 asked for outcomes

The 2022 plan's 26 KPIs measure results: pavement condition, sidewalk safety, injuries, retrofits, canopy. The studio's schema stores budget, years and two yes/no flags. The referral asks for "schedules and their budgets." Neither connects a project to the indicator it is supposed to move. A tracker built to the referral's wording would show whether money was spent on time. It would not show whether streets got better. High on the content of each; the reading is ours.

3. Three scoring systems are now in play, plus a fourth proposal

Vision 2050's 60/40 Envision/community scorecard (2022, by asset category); the six-factor rubric used to build the Measure U project list (May 2026: health, life and safety 20; condition, accessibility and resilience 20; community use and equity 20; green resiliency and sustainability 20; deliverability and readiness 10; external funding 10); and the Budget Referral tiers and Public Works priority labels in the CIP. The studio proposes a fourth, without weights, and recommends against "readiness," which the Measure U rubric weights at 10. The referral does not say which one the Program Plan update should use. High on each system as published.

4. The studio did not use the Vision 2050 documents that bear most on its subject

Its only Vision 2050 sources are the 2020 Framework and the December 2025 TF packet. It does not cite the 2022 Program Plan's tracking tools, KPI table or semi-annual reporting, nor the 2022 SAMP or NexGen. It treats the 60/40 scorecard as a project-ranking tool. The 2022 plan says plainly that the scorecard ranked asset categories and that project ranking would come later. High

5. Vision 2050's own milestones are the referral's first test

The TF set a Program Plan update for Spring 2026, an AMP steering committee and the start of condition assessment for July 2026, and an AMP for four asset classes by July 2027. The referral's first semi-annual report falls around late March 2027. If it does not say where those four milestones stand, the reporting cycle is not tracking Vision 2050. High on the dates; Moderate on the report date (six months from adoption; no date is set).

DThe counterargument

Strongest case for the referral as written

Vision 2050 is a long-range framework whose project machinery was designed to switch on with new funding. Without that funding, the honest course is to restart from the CIP the City actually adopts, and that is what the referral does. Tracking spending and schedule comes first because outcome indicators mean little until project data exists. The studio produced a working tracker template in one semester; the 2022 plan's website is now offline. Asking for less and getting it beats re-adopting 26 KPIs no one reports. Moderate

Assessment

The sequencing argument is right: a project ledger has to exist before outcomes can be attached to it. But the referral's wording would lock in a schedule-and-budget tracker as the definition of done. The KPI table and the targets are already adopted. Adding two fields to each project record would connect the dashboard to Vision 2050 rather than run parallel to it: the Table 9 indicator the project serves, and the baseline condition it addresses. That costs a column. Deferring it turns the "transparency hub" into a spending ledger with a map. Moderate; this is a judgment, not a finding.

EQuestions for the first semi-annual report

  • Which scoring system will the updated Program Plan use: the 2022 60/40 scorecard, the Measure U six-factor rubric, or something new? With what weights?
  • Will the dashboard carry, for each project, the Table 9 KPI it serves and a baseline condition value?
  • Where do the TF's July 2026 milestones stand: AMP steering committee, condition assessment start?
  • Will NexGen condition data be published by asset class?
  • Will the dashboard be built from the CIP's structured data (project codes, fund splits, department priority), or re-keyed from the PDF as the prototype was?
  • What became of BerkeleyVision2050.org, and will the dashboard replace it?
