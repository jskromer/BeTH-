What Berkeley Owes Its Own Infrastructure

Berkeley carries two large unfunded obligations: a deferred capital and maintenance backlog, and an unfunded pension and retiree-health liability. This page sets out what each one is, by asset class, where the figures come from, and how fast each is growing. Both counters are derived from published City documents; the arithmetic behind every digit is on the page.

DPension and retiree health

A different kind of obligation from the capital backlog: a legal debt to people that accrues interest at a set rate, rather than a physical debt to assets. The two are kept separate on this page and are never added together. Figures are from the FY2025 ACFR.

Berkeley pays $47.9M a year above normal cost against a $641.1M pension shortfall. $44.2M of that is interest at the 6.90% discount rate, so $3.7M reduces the balance — 0.57% of it a year. Employer contributions to retiree health cover 71.6% of the employer normal cost, so that balance rises. Net of the two, the whole pension and retiree-health side changes by $344K a year.

GASB permits a one-year-lagged measurement date for pensions. Pension figures are measured at 2024-06-30 (valuation 2023-06-30); OPEB is measured at 2025-06-30. Both are as carried on the 2025-06-30 Statement of Net Position, which is how the City reports them. This excludes investment performance and one-off assumption or benefit changes, which dominate any single year; both balances fell by more than these figures in the reported period, on exactly those items.

Berkeley has adopted a pavement target and never published its cost. The Vision 2050 Program Plan, adopted 2022-07-26 sets a target of PCI more than 70, with no target year. Separately, the adopted Street Rehabilitation 5-Year Plan FY2024-FY2028 states that its own funding level does not reach the good tier.

Where the money goes

Of $18M a year, $10M — 55.6% — is absorbed by escalation on the $250M of work still outstanding. Net progress against the published backlog is $8M a year, which clears that backlog around 2046, holding scope constant.

This answers how long to clear the backlog as published, not how long to reach "good". Pavement that is adequate today and slips into the repair queue tomorrow is not counted in the starting balance. The real horizon is longer, by an amount only the City's pavement model can settle.

GMethod and sources

Every figure is produced by a small Python model in this repository (tools/liability_clock/) from the data files in data/liability-clock/. The page renders what the model emits; it computes nothing of its own except advancing the counter between frames. python3 tools/liability_clock/test_golden.py checks 48 invariants, including that every pending accrual contributes exactly zero, that the class table reconciles to the counter, and that the pension figures are never folded into the capital headline.

Identified unfunded need ties to the source workbook exactly ($941,100,000).
