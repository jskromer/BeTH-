The UC Berkeley CIP Studio: A Complete Data Review

The September 29, 2026 referral asks City staff to build an infrastructure dashboard "akin to" a UC Berkeley student prototype. This page lists every source the students cited, separates the data they actually loaded from the material they only read, and checks each of their 16 project records against the adopted Capital Improvement Program.

AWhat was retrieved, and how

The previous version of this review could not open the report's appendices or the policy brief. Both are Google Docs and both export as plain text, so everything below comes from full text. High

  • Report. Improving the CIP Prioritization and Tracking Process in Berkeley, February 2026: full text including Appendices A–F and all 82 footnotes. Google Doc.
  • Policy brief. One page, no data, no figures. Two recommendations: adopt a unified scoring system, and keep developing the dashboard, including "integration with future capital planning." The referral uses almost the same words. Google Doc.
  • Studio website. Five pages (home, full report, policy recommendations, dashboard, team). They embed the two documents and the dashboard and hold no data of their own. Google Site.
  • ArcGIS account. A search for the owning account returns exactly 10 items: 1 dashboard, 1 web map, 1 uploaded shapefile, and 7 hosted feature services (3 project layers, 3 read-only views of them, 1 district layer). Every layer was queried through its REST endpoint on October 22, 2026.
  • The adopted CIP. FY2025–2029 CIP PDF, downloaded and searched for each of the 16 projects in both the summary tables and the individual project sheets.

Budgets: all 16 stored figures equal the CIP's Project Total, a life-of-project figure that includes spending before FY2024. That is not the five-year CIP figure and not an annual budget, and the layer does not say which it is. Summing the 16 rows gives $70.6M; removing the two duplicates gives $56.1M. High

Timelines: the CIP project sheets have no schedule, start-date or completion-date field. Each has a budget by phase (planning, design, construction) and by fiscal year. The stored "timeline" therefore had to be inferred, and 6 of 16 inferences do not match the years the CIP funds. "Prior" years are not broken out by year in the CIP, so a start year before FY2024 cannot be checked. High on the comparison; Moderate that the students inferred the range rather than taking it from another document, since the report names no other source.

DWhat the CIP holds that the prototype dropped

Available in every CIP project sheet, not carried

  • Project code (e.g. PWT1CB2201). Without it the layer cannot be joined back to City financial systems.
  • Department and Department Priority (Critical, Upgrade, Maintain, Desirable, Mandated). The report criticizes these labels as unexplained, then leaves them out of the data.
  • Project Status (New or Continuing).
  • Funding by fund (e.g. 511 T1 Capital, 011 General Fund, 134 Measure BB, 611 Sanitary Sewer, 608 Marina). Several of the 16 are multi-fund. The report recommends a funding-status display; the layer has no funding field.
  • Budget by phase (planning, design, construction) and by year.
  • Location text and Linkage (the plan the project serves: Strategic Plan, Strategic Transportation Plan, Vision Zero).
  • Impact on operating budget.

Source: CIP project sheets, e.g. South Berkeley Senior Center and Southside Complete Streets. High

Fields in the prototype with no CIP source

  • ontime, onbudget: Yes / No / N/A by hand. The CIP has no on-time or on-budget field and no schedule to measure against. On time: 9 Yes, 7 N/A. On budget: 12 Yes, 2 No, 2 N/A. Nothing in the report says how any of these were decided. High
  • cip_score: empty for all 16. No Berkeley score exists to load: the report proposes metrics and deliberately sets no weights. High
  • contact: empty for all 16. The CIP names departments, not people. High
  • proj_cat: 7 categories used. The CIP has 12 program categories; the report says "ten" and lists nine. High

FWhat this means for the referral

Findings

  • The prototype's data is a hand transcription of a public PDF. The budget figures are accurate, but the field holds a life-of-project total and the layer doesn't label it as one.
  • The two status flags the dashboard actually displays rest on no source the report names. The CIP has nothing to measure on-time against.
  • The CIP already publishes the code, department, priority, status, fund split and plan linkage for every project. A real tracker could start from that, as structured data, without new collection.
  • The report's own peer review sets a higher bar than its prototype meets. That is normal for a proof of concept, and it is why "akin to" sets no standard.

High on each fact; the reading is ours.

The counterargument

This is a one-semester student studio, and it says plainly that the data layer is unbuilt. The students chose to show a working template rather than a complete dataset, and the hand-entered flags are placeholders that show what the display would do. Grading a template on its data misses the point of it. Any fair criticism belongs with the referral, which cites the template as a model without saying which data standard the City's version must meet. Moderate

Records to request: the CIP in native spreadsheet or database form, with project codes, so a tracker can be built from structured data rather than a PDF.
