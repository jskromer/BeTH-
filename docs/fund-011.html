The General Fund: where the surplus went

The one fund the Council controls. For seven years its budget has projected a 'structural deficit' while the audited year came in under budget. Two different things: FY2024 and FY2025 below are audited actuals; the deficit and the balancing actions are budget projections. The ledger shows money transferred out to capital, reserves and the pension trust, and other money scheduled in from internal-service balances to close the projected gap.

Bottom line

Transferred out to some funds, scheduled back in from others.

Audited actuals: in FY2024 the General Fund transferred $41.1M out, more than budgeted, into capital, the pension trust and reserves, and its balance fell for the first time since 2018; in FY2025 it moved $33.9M out and $9.0M in. Budgeted, not yet actual: the FY2026 AAO schedules $23.5M of transfers in, including $5.2M from the Workers' Compensation fund and $6.5M from the IT cost-allocation fund, to close a gap the City calls structural. These are different funds from the FY2024 destinations, so this is not the same money coming back, and nothing here establishes that it is. They are transfers, which carry no obligation to repay, not borrowing. The General Fund did lend in FY2025, but separately and at a different scale: $2,477,099 due from the Marina Operations Fund at June 30, 2025, a receivable rather than a transfer. The surplus is audited history; the deficit is a forward projection; the transfers are what the budget uses to close the projected gap.

How the fund was set up

The General Fund receives the taxes and fees that are not earmarked: property tax, sales tax, business license, transfer tax, and the City's share of fines and charges. It is about a third of the all-funds budget and the only fund the Council can redirect without a vote of the people. Its balance was $154.9M at June 30, 2025, of which $31.7M was unassigned. High

$18,156,996 of the $19,679,684 shown as capital outlay is a lease or subscription signed, not spending paid out. Under GASB 87 and 96 the City records the whole multi-year commitment in the year it signs: as an expenditure inside capital outlay, and as an other financing source of the same amount. No cash changes hands in that entry, and the City takes on a liability for the future payments. What the ledger shows

Finding 1

The fund under-spends and transfers out the difference.

FY2024 transfers out were $41.1M against revenue of $261.8M and spending of $241.8M; FY2025 transfers out were $33.9M. The destinations are capital projects ($14.1M in FY2025), the catastrophic and public-liability insurance funds ($10.0M), the PERS savings fund ($4.8M) and reserves. The unassigned balance fell from $70M (2023) to $29.6M (2024) by reclassification, not spending. High

Finding 2

The FY2026 fix reverses the direction.

The FY2026 AAO schedules $23.5M of transfers into the General Fund: $6.5M from the IT cost-allocation fund, $6.0M from the payroll deduction trust, $5.2M from the Workers' Compensation fund, $2.6M from health realignment, $2.5M of Measure U1 housing money, $1.0M from parking meters. Three of those are internal-service balances that exist to pay future claims and replacements. High

Finding 3

Seven years, seven one-time closes.

The budget's own deficit history: $10M (FY2019), $40M, $27M, $22M, $12M, $17M, $29M (FY2026), each closed with reserves, ARPA, fund balance, vacancy savings, the Section 115 trust, or a Workers' Comp holiday. FY2027 adds $12.1M of one-time money (operating reserve and the dissolved Successor Agency) pending the November 2026 sales tax. High

Finding 4

The General Fund is the Marina's lender and its landlord.

It lent the Marina Fund $2.48M in FY2025 (recorded as due from), transferred $1.3M plus $226K in FY2026, collects the Marina's indirect charges, and under Resolution 70,997 forgoes $1.0-1.5M a year of Waterfront hotel tax to repay the Workers' Compensation loan. High

The counterargument

Strongest case against this page

Transferring surplus to capital, reserves and the pension trust is what prudent cities do; the Auditor criticized Berkeley for under-funding exactly those things. A structural deficit measured on the operating base is real even when fund balance is high, because the balance is one-time and the costs recur. And drawing internal-service balances in a crisis year is a recognized bridge, provided the charges resume. The ledger shows the sweep and the draw-back; whether that is discipline or circularity depends on whether the charges resume in FY2027. Moderate
