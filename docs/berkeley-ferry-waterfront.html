Pier, Ferry & Marina Parking: Where the Waterfront Decisions Stand

Two linked decisions are moving at the Berkeley waterfront: a ~$105M rebuilt pier with an all-electric ferry landing, and a paid-parking pilot on three Marina lots. Since August, the parking permit was elevated to a full hearing and then pulled from the calendar; the Governor certified the ferry project for fast-track CEQA litigation; and the City is answering comments on the Draft EIR. This page lays out what each step actually does.

AThe paid-parking pilot at BCDC

The Marina is mostly built on Bay fill, so changes to public access there need a BCDC permit. The City applied for a limited-term paid-parking program on three lots: South Cove East, South Cove West, and J&K.

What people raised at BCDC

  • BCDC's own policies favor maximizing free, open shoreline access.
  • Scope of BCDC jurisdiction over this area of fill.
  • Piecemealing: the three-lot pilot is one part of a larger parking plan tied to the ferry project, and was presented alone.

Source: participant notes; themes match the commenter list. Moderate

The City's side of the ledger

  • The Marina Fund has run operating deficits for years and has relied on General Fund balancing.
  • Its biggest revenue source, the Doubletree lease, is uncertain.
  • Docks and pilings carry roughly $131M of unfunded capital need.

Source: Jan 27 2026 Marina fee staff report; this site's cross-fund page. High

BThe Governor's CEQA certification — what it does

On Sept 16, 2026 the Governor certified the "Berkeley Water Transportation Pier Ferry Project" under SB 149. The certification changes how any CEQA lawsuit would be handled. The project itself still needs a certified Final EIR, Council approvals, and BCDC and other permits.

QuestionAnswerConfidence
ApplicantsCity of Berkeley and San Francisco Bay Ferry (WETA)High
Litigation timelineAny CEQA challenge to the EIR or approvals "must be resolved, to the extent feasible, within 270 days" of the certified record being filed with the court. LCI describes the usual timeline as 3–5 years. The 270 days is a target with a feasibility qualifier.High
GHG conditionFor transportation projects the law requires no net additional GHG emissions, excluding construction-worker commuting (PRC §21189.83(b)). Emissions are mitigated first by direct reductions, then by offsets in the same air district, then by regional offsets with specific local benefit — backed by a binding commitment between lead agency and applicant.High
Other conditionsMust advance the state transportation climate action plan, qualify as a public work, and include a binding agreement to mitigate impacts in disadvantaged communities. Capped at 10 local/regional transportation projects statewide, first come first served.High
Public recordThe administrative record must be prepared and posted as it develops — that is the purpose of berkeleypierferryproject.com.High
Claimed benefitsGovernor's office: ~19,000 vehicle miles removed per day; ~60% lower potential cancer risk in portside communities; $2.31B in health benefits. These are the applicants' projections, not independently checked here.Moderate
TimingThe certification came before the Final EIR. The GHG and mitigation commitments will need to match whatever the Final EIR finds.High on sequence

CThe Draft EIR and what comes next

Feb 27, 2026. DEIR released (5 volumes). The notice set a 45-day review ending Apr 13; the City's project page shows the period running to Apr 28 (60 days). Public comment meeting Mar 16. High on release; Moderate on the extension.
Significant and unavoidable impacts identified in the DEIR: construction noise inconsistent with the Berkeley Municipal Code (including pile driving); cumulative construction impacts; and possible cumulative roadway noise on University Avenue. Moderate — from the notice summary; check the DEIR text.
Comments. City staff reportedly told participants roughly 16,000 itemized comments came in, each of which the Final EIR must answer. BWCC submitted a ~100-page letter. No official count has been published. Unknown
Next. Final EIR and use-permit votes at Council: "winter 2026" per the Berkeley Scanner; "this fall" per the Daily Californian. Design at ~60%, 90% expected this fall; construction from 2028 over two summers. Moderate
