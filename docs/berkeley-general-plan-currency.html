The General Plan Clock

Berkeley's General Plan has nine elements. One of them has been updated in the last twenty-four years. This page records what each element says, when it was last touched, and which state requirements were built to fire on an update that never came.

1. What is adopted

verified The operative Berkeley General Plan was adopted December 18, 2001 and April 23, 2002. It carries the seven state-required elements, organized into nine Berkeley elements. Source: 2024 General Plan Annual Progress Report, City of Berkeley, submitted March 21, 2025, p. 3.

verified The Transportation Element serves as Berkeley's circulation element. It was adopted December 18, 2001. It contains Policy T-4, the Transit-First Policy, and four network maps: Vehicular Circulation, Transit, Bicycle Circulation, and Emergency Access and Evacuation. The 2000 Bicycle Plan is incorporated into it by reference.

verified Its factual baseline is the 1993 report Conditions, Trends and Issues: A Background Report for Updating the City's General Plan. Its commute-mode figures are from the 1990 census. Its traffic projections run from 1990 to 2020. The planning horizon of Berkeley's operative circulation element expired six years ago.

3. The circulation element and the triggers that never fired

California modernized the circulation element three times since Berkeley adopted its own. Each round of reform is written as a trigger, not a deadline. The obligation attaches when a city substantively revises the element. A city that never revises never incurs it.

StatuteWhat it requiresWhen it attachesStatus in Berkeley
AB 1358 (2008)
Complete Streets Act		 Modify the circulation element to plan a balanced, multimodal network serving bicyclists, children, people with disabilities, motorists, freight, pedestrians, transit users and seniors. Commencing Jan 1, 2011, "upon any substantive revision of the circulation element." Gov. Code § 65302(b)(2)(A). Not triggered. assumption
SB 743 (2013)
CEQA transportation metric		 Replace automobile delay and LOS with VMT as the CEQA transportation impact metric. Statewide by July 1, 2020, through CEQA Guidelines § 15064.3. Applies to environmental review, not to the general plan directly. Handled outside the General Plan. verified
SB 932 (2022) Incorporate the Federal Highway Administration Safe System Approach; develop bicycle, pedestrian and traffic calming plans; identify safety corridors; set traffic calming around schools, parks and business districts; complete all identified actions within 25 years; begin implementation within 2 years of adoption. "Upon any substantive revision of the circulation element, on or after January 1, 2025." Not triggered. assumption

How Berkeley handled SB 743

verified Berkeley met the CEQA-side obligation with a standalone staff document, City of Berkeley VMT Criteria and Thresholds, dated June 29, 2020. That document states the City would also update its Transportation Impact Study Guidelines. It is an environmental-review instrument. It did not amend the Transportation Element, and the Transportation Element still frames Berkeley's transportation problem in the congestion terms the state abandoned.

assumption The reading, stated plainly so it can be argued with: Berkeley is not in violation of AB 1358 or SB 932. Both are trigger-based, and Berkeley has not pulled the trigger in twenty-five years. The observation is not non-compliance. It is that the state's chosen mechanism for modernizing circulation elements is a voluntary trip-wire, and a city can hold a 1990-baseline circulation element indefinitely by declining to touch it, while adopting every downstream plan the modern statutes would have required anyway.

Where the policy actually lives instead

Berkeley has not stopped making transportation policy. It has made it in instruments that sit below the General Plan and are not bound by its consistency requirement: the Berkeley Strategic Transportation Plan (2016), the Bicycle Plan, the Pedestrian Plan (2020), the Vision Zero Action Plan (2020), and the Transit-First Policy Implementation Plan (Council-approved March 2023). verified

Two consequences follow, and both are checkable rather than rhetorical:

  • None of those plans re-derived the baseline the General Plan was supposed to hold. They implement a policy framework whose stated conditions data is from 1990 and 1993.
  • verified The CY2024 APR records that the Vision Zero program was "largely on hold during 2023 and 2024," against a Council-adopted goal of eliminating severe injury and fatal traffic crashes by 2028. The Telegraph Corridor Study and Adeline Complete Streets were each on hold during 2023 and restarted in 2024. A goal with a date, a program on hold for two of the intervening years, and no element-level reporting obligation is the pattern this Hub exists to record.

assumption Under SB 1035, a safety element is to be reviewed and revised as necessary upon each housing element revision and upon each LHMP revision, and not less than once every eight years. Berkeley revised its housing element in January 2023 and its LHMP in March 2025. Whether the city treats those as satisfied by the in-progress update, or as review obligations that were separately met, is a question for the City Attorney and Planning, not something this page should assert.

5. Internal consistency

verified Government Code § 65300.5 requires that a general plan and its elements comprise an integrated, internally consistent and compatible statement of policies. SB 932 amended that section in 2022 to underline the point.

Berkeley now carries a 2023 housing element sized to an 8,934-unit RHNA, a 2025 middle-housing rezoning, an AB 2011 approval of 739 homes at North Berkeley BART, and a corridors upzoning in progress, all sitting on a circulation element whose conditions data predates every one of them.

assumption This is a litigation surface, not a proven defect. A general-plan consistency challenge requires a specific inconsistency between a specific approval and a specific policy, and courts give cities wide deference. The honest statement is that the exposure exists and has not been tested, not that Berkeley's plan is invalid.

The specific inconsistency worth checking first

The 2001 Transportation Element commits the city to neighborhood traffic calming and neighborhood protection on residential streets.

The city's own 2025 Evacuation Time Study found that permanent traffic calming devices, including circles, speed humps and diverters, slow evacuation flow, and recommended the city develop a citywide traffic flow and evacuation strategy addressing movable versus permanent calming measures.

Those two positions have not been reconciled in any adopted document, because reconciling them would require amending the element. to pull Confirm the calming finding against the primary study text before using it.

6. The evacuation study, and what it says about the flatlands

Berkeley commissioned a 323-page Evacuation Time Study from KLD Associates, released July 2025, at a reported cost of $600,000, funded from Measure FF (2020), the General Fund and other sources. The tsunami scenarios are the west and northwest Berkeley scenarios. The Max Phase evacuation zone runs from the Bay to Seventh Street. This is not a hypothetical exercise for the flatlands: verified on December 5, 2024 the National Tsunami Warning Center issued a warning covering Berkeley, and the City activated its Emergency Operations Center and conducted evacuations of the waterfront and areas in west Berkeley. The warning was cancelled roughly an hour after it was issued.

Other reported findings, each worth confirming against the primary text:

  • Roughly 1,600 residential parcels citywide have only one way in or out.
  • New housing of all types increased modeled evacuation times by over 100% in some scenarios.
  • Removing parked cars from narrow roadways produced far less improvement than expected. Selective restrictions may still help emergency vehicles move inbound against traffic.
  • Recommendations include adaptive traffic signals, battery backups on all signals, and a citywide traffic flow and evacuation strategy.

verified Separately, the CY2024 APR records that during the October 2024 extreme fire weather event the City advised hills residents to leave in advance, "because a fire starting under those conditions could quickly become catastrophic and the hills' roadway infrastructure will not support a quick evacuation." That is the city's own assessment of its road network, stated in a report filed with the state, and it appears in the Disaster Preparedness section rather than the Transportation section.

7. The four questions, applied

This site's standing method, applied to the General Plan rather than to a tax measure.

QuestionAnswer on record
What did it promise?Six stated objectives, including maintaining and improving transit service and reducing automobile use and VMT in Berkeley.
What would success have looked like?No target values, no target year, and no baseline other than 1990 and 1993 conditions data.
What do we observe?The APR reports activity: grants applied for, projects designed, corridors studied. It reports no outcome measured against any stated objective. Transit ridership, mode share and VMT do not appear.
What is the review period and sunset?An annual progress report is required by Gov. Code § 65400. There is no target to report against, no sunset, and no reauthorization.

The APR is a compliance document that discharges a reporting duty without reporting an outcome. That is the same structure this Hub has recorded on the revenue side: Berkeley reports thoroughly on activity and spending, and has no instrument anywhere that asks whether the stated objective was met.
