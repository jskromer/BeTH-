Following the money

Workers' Compensation: two years of zero revenue

An internal-service fund that is supposed to bill departments for self-insured injury claims. In FY2024 and FY2025 it billed nothing, lent $5.5M to the Marina docks, and ended FY2025 $9.75M in the hole. The FY2026 budget then moves another $5.2M out of it to the General Fund.

Bottom line

A claims fund used as a piggy bank.

Operating revenue: $0 in FY2024, $0 in FY2025. Claims and expenses: $7.8M then $12.0M. Transfers out: $5.5M to the Marina dock project in FY2024, $5.2M to the General Fund budgeted for FY2026. Net position went from +$1.9M to −$9.75M in one year. The 'holidays' the Marina decks mention are this: departments, the General Fund among them, stopped paying their workers' compensation charge, and the fund paid claims out of its balance.

01

How the fund was set up

The Workers' Compensation Fund is one of eight internal service funds. Departments are charged a premium set in the budget; the fund pays injured-worker claims and administration. Its balance is the cushion against claims that take years to settle. The ACFR's self-insurance note carries the actuarial claims liability. High

What the ledger shows

Finding 1

Zero charges, two years running.

The FY2024 and FY2025 statements both show operating revenue of $0 for this fund. The FY2025 deficit history calls FY2025 a 'Workers Comp holiday'; the Marina decks describe the same holiday as Marina relief. Every department that pays into this fund, including General Fund departments, got the relief. High

Finding 2

The $5.5M Marina loan was booked as a transfer out.

Resolution 70,997 (July 25, 2023) called it an internal loan repayable from Waterfront hotel tax. The FY2024 ACFR shows it as a $5,500,000 transfer out, with no receivable, and the Marina Operations Fund shows no loan in. Where the money went on the books, and whether any TOT has come back, are open questions. High on the records; Unknown on the reconciliation

Finding 3

The General Fund is next.

The FY2026 AAO budgets a $5,187,019 transfer from this fund to the General Fund, from a fund whose audited net position was already −$9.75M. High

09

The counterargument

Strongest case against this page

A self-insurance fund can carry a negative net position for a time because claims are paid over years, and an actuary, not the balance sheet, says whether it is adequately funded. Suspending charges when a fund is over-reserved is a legitimate way to return money to departments; the City Auditor has in the past criticized over-accumulation in internal-service funds. If charges resume in FY2027 at an actuarially set rate, two holiday years are a timing choice, not a loss. Moderate
