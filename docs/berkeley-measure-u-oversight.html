Measure U: Who Watches the $300 Million, and What Vision 2050 Already Says About Money

Measure U's text gives two City commissions an annual reporting role and the City Auditor a review at least every three years. Five Vision 2050 documents, written between 2020 and 2025, and the September 29, 2026 asset management referral set out how the City said it would plan, prioritize and finance infrastructure. This page lays the two side by side.

AWhat the measure text says about oversight

SectionWhat it provides
6.A–6.CBonds finance "the acquisition or improvement of real property"; proceeds are used only for the measure's purposes and held in an account the City creates.
6.DProceeds may reimburse the City for General Fund or other advances made for Improvements before bond proceeds are available.
6.EThe City Manager gives Council an annual report on bond receipts, expenditures and project status, as part of the budget process.
6.FThe PRW (parks, recreation and waterfront Improvements) and T&I (transportation and other public infrastructure) commissions each report to Council annually on whether expenditures match the bonds' purposes, and make recommendations on projects.
6.GAll expenditures are subject to an audit by the City Auditor at least once every three years.
RecitalsBond funding "should be guided by" a non-exhaustive project list from the 2026 public input process, and projects not on that list are also eligible if they consist of the acquisition or improvement of real property for public infrastructure and facilities. The recitals also recognize "adequate maintenance funding from existing sources" and say proceeds are not to supplant existing City infrastructure funding.

The text establishes no separate citizens' oversight committee beyond the two commissions. High on the wording, read from the City's full-text PDF. The commissions "report" and "recommend"; the text does not use the word "audit" for their role.

BWhat the measure text does not say

  • It states no maximum tax rate per $100,000 of assessed value, no final maturity and no sale schedule. Those sit in the Tax Rate Statement (Resolution 72,338-N.S., Exhibit B). A fact sheet by John Gage that cites that statement reports three $100M sales (2027, 2032, 2037) and a rate peaking near $35 per $100,000 from FY2040-41. This page has not re-read the statement. Moderate
  • It does not mention vehicles or state whether operating costs may be paid from proceeds. Section 6.B limits proceeds to the measure's listed purposes. High
  • It does not name a standard against which the commissions evaluate project lists. High

EQuestions this raises for the commissions and staff

  • Which single need figure will the updated Program Plan use, and how does it reconcile with the figures above?
  • Which scoring system governs project selection: the 60/40 scorecard, the six-factor Measure U rubric, or a new one?
  • What is the baseline of existing infrastructure funding against which "non-supplanting" will be tested each year?
  • For each project, what are the annual operating and maintenance cost and the fund that pays it?
  • How does the repayment term of each sale compare with the useful life of what it buys?
  • Were the 2021–22 bonding-capacity and revenue-options studies delivered, and where are they?
  • Where do the Realize Vision 2050 milestones stand (Program Plan update, bonding strategy, AMP steering committee, condition assessments)?
  • Will the Item 26 dashboard carry Measure U projects, and with what fields?
