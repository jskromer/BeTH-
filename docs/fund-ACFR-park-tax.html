The Parks Tax: what Measure Y bought

A parcel tax for parks, trees and landscaping, raised 20% by voters in November 2024. Its first-year effect includes $1.58M a year of Waterfront costs moved off the Marina Fund.

Bottom line

A parks tax that now carries the Waterfront.

Measure Y raised the Parks Tax by about $3.8M a year. From July 2025, $1.58M a year of Waterfront park and landscape maintenance moved from the failing Marina Fund onto this one, and in FY2027 5% of the Waterfront Deputy Director follows. The ballot question named parks generally; the Council's September 2024 intent resolution named the Waterfront. The shift was disclosed, in a resolution rather than on the ballot.

01

How the fund was set up

The Parks Tax is a special parcel tax, first approved in 1997 and raised in 2000, 2008, 2014 and 2024, charged per square foot of building improvements and indexed annually. By law it can be spent only on parks, trees and landscaping. It is the second-largest source of parks money after the General Fund. High

Finding 2

The Marina shift is a third of the Measure Y uplift.

Measure Y adds about $3.8M a year; the Waterfront cost shift takes $1.58M of it. Whether the rest raised park maintenance capacity or absorbed other cost growth is not visible until the FY2026 audit. High on the figures; Moderate on the reading

09

The counterargument

Strongest case against this page

Cesar Chavez, Shorebird and Horseshoe are parks, and the Parks Tax is for parks; charging their upkeep to the parks tax rather than to boat-slip fees corrects a 60-year anomaly rather than creating one. The Council said so in a public resolution before the vote, and 75% of voters approved. The ledger records the shift; it does not say it was wrong. Moderate
