Equipment Maintenance & Replacement: the fund that gets raided

Two internal-service funds, reported as one: departments pay 672 to maintain vehicles and 671 to replace them. The 2021 audit titled its report 'Fleet Replacement Fund Short Millions.' Since then $2.5M has moved from the replacement fund to pay maintenance.

Bottom line

Replacement money, spent on repairs.

In FY2024 $500,000 and in FY2025 $2,000,000 moved out of the replacement fund (671) to cover the maintenance fund's (672) operating costs. The 2021 audit asked for an Administrative Regulation requiring written justification before any such transfer; in October 2025 it was still 'partly implemented.' The combined fund's balance is healthy on paper, $39.8M, because it includes vehicles not yet bought.

01

How the fund was set up

Departments pay an annual charge per vehicle into fund 672 for maintenance and into fund 671 to accumulate the vehicle's replacement cost. When the vehicle ages out, 671 buys the replacement. The ACFR reports the two as one internal service fund. The City Auditor's 2021 report found the replacement charges too low to replace the fleet on schedule. High

What the ledger shows

Finding 1

$2.5M moved from replacement to maintenance in two years.

Per the February 27, 2025 fleet audit presentation: $500,000 in FY2024 and $2,000,000 in FY2025 from fund 671 to fund 672 for operating expenses. The audit's recommendation 1.6 (an A.R. requiring documented justification before raiding the fund) was still partly implemented in October 2025. High

Finding 2

The fund replaces about 7% of the fleet a year.

FY2020-24 average: 46 units a year, 7.4% of the fleet, implying a 13-14 year replacement cycle. $50.3M was spent or encumbered on replacement from FY2019 to FY2025, and the 671 balance rose from $7.4M to $19.7M. High

Finding 3

Electrification is an unfunded step change.

Staff estimate about $9.0M of extra vehicle cost plus $6.5M of chargers for the Zero Waste fleet alone (October 2025). A new maintenance rate model is promised for the FY2027-28 mid-biennial update. Moderate

09

The counterargument

Strongest case against this page

Moving replacement money to maintenance keeps vehicles running when the maintenance charge is set too low, and the combined fund still ran a surplus both years with a $39.8M net position. The audit's complaint is about documentation and rate-setting, not insolvency. A fund with rising balances and 11 of 12 recommendations partly implemented is a slow fix, not a raid. Moderate
